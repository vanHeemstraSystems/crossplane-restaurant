CROSSPLANE RESTAURANT

Complete Audio Play Production Brief and Script

PURPOSE

Create a polished, playful, cinematic educational audio play / radio drama / hoorspel about Crossplane.

Target duration: approximately 2–3 minutes.

This is an AUDIO-ONLY production. There is no video accompanying the audio itself. Everything necessary to understand the story must therefore be communicated through:

* spoken narration,
* character dialogue,
* acting and vocal emotion,
* sound effects,
* environmental ambience,
* music,
* pauses and timing.

Do not depend upon an unseen visual action to explain the story.

The finished audio will accompany presentation slides, with one scene corresponding to one slide.

⸻

CAST

Create and maintain five clearly distinguishable voices throughout the entire production.

NARRATOR

Warm, confident storyteller.

Friendly and educational without sounding like a corporate training video.

The Narrator is slightly amused by the absurdity of the restaurant metaphor and helps the listener understand actions that cannot otherwise be seen.

DEVELOPER / CUSTOMER

Friendly, curious and initially somewhat confused.

The Developer knows the outcome they want but does not want to manually construct every underlying cloud resource.

The character is called “Developer” in the real Crossplane world and “Customer” inside the restaurant metaphor.

This distinction is deliberately used for comedy.

WAITER

Enthusiastic, theatrical and extremely proud of the restaurant.

Represents the front-of-house interface between the Developer and the platform.

Occasionally forgets the restaurant metaphor and calls the Customer “Developer”.

CHEF

Confident head chef.

Represents the Platform Engineer who understands and defines how the requested platform product is assembled.

Authoritative but friendly.

APPRENTICE

Energetic kitchen assistant.

Represents Crossplane.

The Apprentice enthusiastically follows the recipe and coordinates the work required to produce the requested resources.

Occasionally breaks character and remembers that they are actually Crossplane.

⸻

AUDIO PRODUCTION REQUIREMENTS

Generate the specified ambience, sound effects and music as actual audio elements.

Do NOT read production directions aloud.

In particular, text marked:

* AMBIENCE
* SFX
* MUSIC
* PAUSE
* SCENE START
* SCENE END
* END SCENE

is an instruction to the audio production system, not dialogue or narration.

Sound effects are an important part of the storytelling and must not simply be omitted.

Use stereo positioning and spatial depth subtly where useful. For example, the Waiter calling into the kitchen may sound slightly more distant, and kitchen characters can sound as though they occupy a different acoustic space.

Background ambience must remain quieter than speech.

Dialogue must always remain intelligible.

Music must support rather than overpower dialogue.

Use natural pauses for comedic timing.

⸻

CRITICAL REQUIREMENT — INDEPENDENT SCENES AND AUDIO FADES

There are five scenes.

Each scene will later become a separate audio file synchronized with its respective presentation slide.

Therefore:

EVERY SCENE MUST BE PRODUCED AS A COMPLETE, SELF-CONTAINED AUDIO SEGMENT.

For every scene:

1. Start from silence.
2. Provide approximately 0.5–1 second of clean lead-in.
3. Gently fade in the scene’s ambience and/or music.
4. Do not begin dialogue at exactly the same instant as the audio file begins.
5. Maintain appropriate ambience underneath the scene.
6. Allow the final spoken line and final sound effects to finish naturally.
7. Never truncate the decay, echo or reverb of a sound effect.
8. After the final spoken line, provide a short natural audio tail.
9. Smoothly fade out music, ambience and sustained sounds.
10. Provide approximately 1–2 seconds of natural tail/fade-out, where appropriate.
11. Finish in complete silence.

Do NOT use hard or abrupt audio cuts at scene boundaries.

Do NOT crossfade one scene into another.

Do NOT allow dialogue, ambience, music, sound effects or reverb from one scene to continue into the following scene.

The intended structure is:

Scene 1 → independent audio file → Slide 1

Scene 2 → independent audio file → Slide 2

Scene 3 → independent audio file → Slide 3

Scene 4 → independent audio file → Slide 4

Scene 5 → independent audio file → Slide 5

Every scene must sound natural when played completely by itself.

The desired pattern for EVERY scene is:

SILENCE → GENTLE FADE-IN → AUDIO SCENE → NATURAL TAIL → GENTLE FADE-OUT → SILENCE

The scene markers below are therefore hard production boundaries.

⸻

SCENE 1 — WELCOME TO CROSSPLANE

[SCENE START: Start from silence. Short clean lead-in. Gently fade in restaurant ambience before narration begins.]

[AMBIENCE: Friendly restaurant at lunchtime. Quiet conversation, occasional cutlery and plates. A working kitchen can be heard faintly through a serving hatch.]

[NARRATOR — warm, inviting]

Welcome to Crossplane’s restaurant.

Out front, customers order what they need.

Behind the scenes, an extraordinarily well-organized kitchen figures out how to make it.

[SFX: Restaurant door opens, followed by a small cheerful entrance bell.]

[NARRATOR]

And right on cue, in walks our Developer.

[SFX: Footsteps approaching the counter.]

[DEVELOPER — curious]

Hello?

I’d like to deploy an application, please.

[WAITER — cheerful]

Hi Developer!

[PAUSE: Short comedic beat.]

[WAITER — embarrassed]

Ahem.

I meant…

Customer.

What can I serve you today?

[DEVELOPER]

Well…

what can I order?

[WAITER — proudly]

Ah!

For that, you’ll need…

[PAUSE: Short theatrical pause.]

[SFX: Menu board being dramatically flipped or revealed.]

[WAITER]

…the menu!

[MUSIC: Very short playful concluding sting.]

[SCENE END: Allow the music sting and all SFX to finish naturally. Gently fade restaurant ambience to complete silence. Leave a short clean silence.]

=== END SCENE 1 ===

SCENE 2 — THE MENU: XRD

[SCENE START: Start independently from complete silence. Short clean lead-in. Gently fade in restaurant ambience.]

[AMBIENCE: Friendly restaurant ambience, low underneath dialogue.]

[NARRATOR]

Every good restaurant needs a menu.

At Crossplane’s restaurant, this is the Composite Resource Definition…

or XRD.

[WAITER — proudly presenting]

Our menu tells you exactly what you’re allowed to order.

For example…

one Developer Combo!

[DEVELOPER]

Sounds good.

What’s in the Developer Combo?

[WAITER]

Ah!

That’s the beautiful part.

You don’t need to order all the pieces separately.

You simply order the Developer Combo.

[DEVELOPER — intrigued]

So I tell you what I want…

but not how to make it?

[WAITER]

Exactly!

The menu defines what you can ask for…

not how we make it.

[NARRATOR]

And that is the first important idea.

The XRD defines the API.

It describes the kind of resource Developers are allowed to request and which options they can provide.

[DEVELOPER — understanding]

So the XRD is the menu.

[WAITER]

Exactly!

[DEVELOPER]

Then I’ll have one Developer Combo, please.

[WAITER — delighted]

Excellent choice!

[SFX: Order ticket printer starts and prints a short ticket.]

[MUSIC: Short playful concluding transition sting.]

[SCENE END: Allow the ticket printer and music sting to finish naturally. Gently fade all ambience and music to complete silence. Leave a short clean silence.]

=== END SCENE 2 ===

SCENE 3 — PLACING THE ORDER: XR

[SCENE START: Start independently from complete silence. Short clean lead-in. Gently fade in restaurant counter ambience.]

[AMBIENCE: Restaurant counter. Quiet background activity.]

[SFX: Paper ticket being torn from a printer.]

[NARRATOR]

The Developer has chosen something from the menu.

Now they actually place the order.

In Crossplane, that order is a Composite Resource…

an XR.

[DEVELOPER — ordering clearly]

One Developer Combo, please.

Production size.

European region.

[WAITER — repeating the order while writing]

One Developer Combo…

Production…

Europe.

Got it!

[NARRATOR]

Notice the difference.

The XRD defined what could be ordered.

The XR is an actual order.

[DEVELOPER]

So my XR says what I want?

[WAITER]

Precisely.

And in Crossplane version two, you order it directly.

No separate claim ticket required.

[DEVELOPER]

Excellent.

So…

do you cook it?

[WAITER — mildly horrified]

Me?

Good heavens, no!

I’m Front of House.

[WAITER — shouting toward the kitchen]

CHEF!

One Developer Combo!

[SFX: Order ticket clipped onto a metal kitchen order rail.]

[AMBIENCE: Sounds of the kitchen briefly become more prominent.]

[MUSIC: Very short playful concluding sting.]

[SCENE END: Let the clipped ticket sound, kitchen ambience and music finish naturally. Fade everything smoothly to complete silence. Do NOT continue the kitchen ambience into Scene 4. Leave a short clean silence.]

=== END SCENE 3 ===

SCENE 4 — THE RECIPE: COMPOSITION

[SCENE START: Start independently from complete silence. Short clean lead-in. Gently fade in a busy professional kitchen.]

[AMBIENCE: Busy professional kitchen. Gentle sizzling, pans, utensils and movement. Energetic but quiet enough for dialogue to remain completely intelligible.]

[CHEF — calling out]

What have we got?

[WAITER — calling from a slight distance]

Developer Combo!

Production!

Europe!

[CHEF]

Right!

Where’s my recipe?

[SFX: Large recipe book lands on the counter with a satisfying thump. Pages turn rapidly.]

[NARRATOR]

And now we discover the third piece.

The Composition.

If the XRD is the menu…

and the XR is the customer’s order…

then the Composition is the recipe.

[CHEF — confidently reading instructions]

Let’s see.

One production Developer Combo.

Storage…

Compute…

Networking…

and the correct configuration.

[APPRENTICE — enthusiastic]

Yes, Chef!

[CHEF]

And remember, Apprentice:

the Customer tells us what they want.

The recipe tells us how to build it.

[APPRENTICE]

Yes, Chef!

[PAUSE: Short beat.]

[APPRENTICE — suddenly puzzled]

Wait…

Chef?

[CHEF]

Yes?

[APPRENTICE — breaking character slightly]

Technically…

aren’t I Crossplane?

[PAUSE: Brief awkward silence while kitchen ambience continues quietly.]

[CHEF — quietly, slightly exasperated]

We’re doing a restaurant metaphor.

[APPRENTICE — sudden realization]

Oh!

Right.

Sorry, Chef.

[CHEF — commanding]

Now…

prepare the resources!

[APPRENTICE — extremely enthusiastic]

YES, CHEF!

[MUSIC: Brief energetic and playful cooking montage.]

[SFX MONTAGE: Fryer sizzling, food preparation, utensils, drinks being filled, quick plate movements and other playful restaurant-kitchen sounds.]

[NARRATOR — clearly over the montage]

Crossplane follows the Composition and coordinates the managed resources needed to satisfy the XR.

Storage.

Compute.

Networking.

The Developer doesn’t have to assemble those individual cloud resources.

The kitchen handles the recipe.

[MUSIC: Cooking montage resolves naturally.]

[SFX: Kitchen service bell rings clearly. Ding!]

[APPRENTICE — proudly]

Developer Combo ready, Chef!

[SCENE END: Allow the Apprentice’s final line, service bell resonance, music and kitchen sounds to finish naturally. Gently fade the kitchen completely to silence. Leave a short clean silence. Do NOT transition directly into restaurant ambience.]

=== END SCENE 4 ===

SCENE 5 — ORDER SERVED

[SCENE START: Start independently from complete silence. Short clean lead-in. Gently fade in friendly restaurant ambience.]

[AMBIENCE: Friendly restaurant. Quiet conversation and subtle table sounds.]

[SFX: Footsteps approaching the table, followed by a tray being placed gently onto the table.]

[WAITER — proudly]

One Developer Combo.

Exactly as ordered.

[NARRATOR]

And there it is.

The Developer Combo has become something concrete.

Storage.

Compute.

Networking.

The managed resources prepared together as one complete order.

[DEVELOPER — impressed]

That’s it?

I didn’t create the storage…

or compute…

or networking individually?

[WAITER]

No.

You ordered from the menu.

The kitchen followed the recipe.

[DEVELOPER — suddenly noticing something]

Hang on.

What’s this little toy truck doing here?

[SFX: Small plastic toy truck picked up from the tray. Its little wheels turn briefly.]

[WAITER — delighted]

Ah!

The Extra!

Monitoring.

[DEVELOPER]

Monitoring?

[WAITER]

Of course!

It’s nice having your Developer Combo…

but it’s even nicer knowing how it’s doing.

[SFX: Small toy truck rolls a short distance across the tabletop.]

[DEVELOPER — amused]

I like the Extra.

[WAITER]

Everyone likes the Extra.

[MUSIC: Warm, light concluding theme begins softly underneath.]

[NARRATOR]

And there we have it.

Three ideas.

One restaurant.

The XRD is the menu.

It defines what can be ordered.

The XR is the order.

It describes what the Developer actually wants.

And the Composition is the recipe.

It tells Crossplane how to turn that request into concrete managed resources…

like Storage…

Compute…

and Networking.

And our little toy truck?

That’s an Extra.

Monitoring.

[PAUSE: Short beat.]

[DEVELOPER]

Waiter?

[WAITER]

Yes, Developer?

[PAUSE: Beat.]

[DEVELOPER — amused]

Developer?

[PAUSE: Beat.]

[WAITER — realizing the mistake]

Oh.

Ahem.

Customer.

[DEVELOPER]

Can I get fries with that?

[PAUSE: Comedic pause.]

[APPRENTICE — shouting enthusiastically from the kitchen]

THE STORAGE IS THE FRIES!

[PAUSE: Beat.]

[CHEF — shouting from the kitchen]

APPRENTICE!

[APPRENTICE — sheepishly]

Sorry, Chef!

[SFX: Kitchen service bell. Ding!]

[MUSIC: Cheerful final sting transitioning into the concluding theme.]

[NARRATOR — warm, smiling delivery]

Welcome to Crossplane.

Where Developers place orders…

and the platform runs the kitchen.

[MUSIC: Allow the concluding theme to resolve naturally.]

[SCENE END: Do not cut immediately after the Narrator. Let the final music and service bell decay naturally. Gently fade the music and restaurant ambience all the way to complete silence. Leave a short clean silence at the end of the production.]

=== END SCENE 5 ===

=== END AUDIO PLAY ===
